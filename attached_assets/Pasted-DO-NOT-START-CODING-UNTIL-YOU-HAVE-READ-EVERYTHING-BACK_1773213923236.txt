BACKGROUND — WHAT IS WRONG WITH THE CURRENT FORMULA

You have correctly identified that the current formula is flawed:

text
utilizationRate = (totalCumulativeRH / hoursElapsed) × 100
where hoursElapsed = wall-clock hours since the earliest audit record for the vessel.

This is wrong because:

Cumulative lifetime hours of a component (e.g., Main Engine at 4,020 hrs) can easily exceed the audit tracking period, causing >100% results that need capping — which makes the metric meaningless

The denominator is shared across all components regardless of whether a component was even running during that period

It mixes lifetime accumulated hours with a calendar elapsed time — these are incompatible concepts

WHAT THE NEW FORMULA MUST BE

The new formula must be period-based:

text
Utilization Rate = (Hours the component ran IN the selected period / Total hours in that period) × 100
Where:

"Hours the component ran in the period" = the sum of running hours added (delta) to this component's RH log during the selected period. This means: look at the running hours audit entries (RH update logs) for this component, filter only those entries within the selected period, and sum up all the delta (hours added) values. Do NOT use cumulative total hours — use only the incremental hours logged in the period.

"Total hours in that period" = fixed calendar hours for the selected period:

Weekly = 168 hours (7 × 24)

Monthly = 720 hours (30 × 24)

Quarterly = 2,160 hours (90 × 24)

Yearly = 8,760 hours (365 × 24)

The result is capped at 100% maximum (in case of data entry errors)

Round to 1 decimal place

Example: Main Diesel Engine. If in the last 30 days, crew logged RH updates totalling 480 hours added → Utilization = 480 / 720 × 100 = 66.7%. This makes sense — the engine ran for 66.7% of the month.
