Final business interpretation to implement
There must be four distinct business stages in the work order lifecycle:

Generation

Planned

Due / Grace P

Overdue

These stages must not be merged.

1. Generation rule is fixed, not vessel-driven
Work order generation must happen at a fixed advance point:

Calendar/date-based jobs: generate WO 30 days before due date

Running-hours-based jobs: generate WO 720 hours before RH due point

This generation trigger is independent of vessel lead time settings.

Important:

Vessel Lead Time must not be used to decide when the WO is generated.

Vessel Lead Time is for deciding when the WO becomes Due, not when it is created.

2. Initial status after generation
When a WO is generated at the fixed advance point above, it must be created in Planned stage.

That means:

Initial effective status should map to the Planned tab.

It must not be born directly as Due.

Generation and Due must become two separate events in the system.

If current status naming internally uses “Active” for Planned tab, that is acceptable as long as:

generated WO enters that state first,

and appears under the Planned tab.

3. Due transition must use vessel Lead Time
After a WO has already been generated and is sitting in Planned, it should move to Due only when it enters the vessel-specific Lead Time window.

Calendar jobs
Due transition must happen when job is within vessel-configured calendar lead days.

Running hours jobs
Due transition must happen when remaining hours are within vessel-configured RH lead hours.

This means vessel settings shown in Admin such as “Lead: 7d / 50hrs” must be used for the Planned -> Due movement, not for the original WO generation. The Admin page explicitly presents vessel-specific Lead Time & Grace Period settings and also separately describes the Company Standard Grace Rule, which confirms these settings are meant to drive later status behavior rather than replace fixed business triggers.

4. Grace logic remains after due date
Once the actual due date or RH due point is crossed:

if the vessel’s grace period is still active,

the WO should continue to show with the existing Grace P marker,

and remain under the Due-side workflow until grace expires.

This grace logic appears already mostly correct in your analysis, so keep it unless any hidden issue is found.

5. Overdue transition after grace expiry
Once grace period is fully exhausted:

WO must become Overdue

WO must show under Overdue tab

This is the final stage.

The required lifecycle is:

WO generated early at fixed threshold

WO shown in Planned

WO moves to Due when within vessel lead time

WO shows Grace P after due date while still inside grace

WO becomes Overdue after grace expiry

In short:

Fixed generation trigger -> Planned -> Vessel lead time triggers Due -> Grace after due date -> Overdue after grace expiry
